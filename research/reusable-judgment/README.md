# Reusable Judgment — the research lineage

*Why lyra keeps a folder about a paste-in ledger. In words; the symbols live
in [FORMULA.md](../../FORMULA.md).*

## The gap, stated in words

A project accumulates decisions about conduct — don't claim done without
verifying, don't put a number in a draft that nothing measured — and each
decision was warranted by something: a correction that actually happened, a
cost that was actually paid. When a new thread applies that decision, it is
running on **inherited judgment**: a stance adopted because of conditions
observed in the past.

The question lyra cares about is the gap between that inherited stance and the
**present warrant** — whether the condition that justified the rule is still
here, in this thread, now. When the condition is present, the inherited rule
is exactly what judgment reusability promises: the correction you don't pay
for twice. When the condition is gone and the rule fires anyway, the same
mechanism manufactures overhang — a stance running on what *was* true,
applied confidently, wearing the authority of policy.

A Judgment Ledger is therefore two experiments in one object. It tests whether
judgment can be made durable across threads, and it tests whether durable
judgment can be made to *let go* — which is why every entry carries its reason
(the expiry condition), its valid exception, and an observable trigger, and
why retirement is a first-class outcome.

## The formula is a pointer here, not an instrument

lyra's residual — one subtraction, defined once in
[FORMULA.md](../../FORMULA.md) — is the research frame for this gap: what is
actually present versus what the default (here, the inherited rule) predicts.
**Pilot 000 does not compute it.** The pilot measures first-pass burden and
blind-labeled reuse events; no residual is calculated, no score is derived
from the equation, and no document in the program presents the formula as a
measurement. The symbolic treatment stays in lyra, as theory the pilot's
evidence may eventually connect to — or fail to.
