# Open questions — Reusable Judgment

*Each question names what would settle it. None is settled. No runs exist.*

## 1. Does the structure do the work, or the judgment?

A pasted ledger is also just more text, and structured text can help for
reasons that have nothing to do with judgment being reusable — priming
carefulness, signaling that the operator has standards, breaking the wall of
prose. **Settled by:** Pilot 000's placebo contrast (arm D vs arm E, a
structure-matched ledger within the preregistered tolerance whose rules govern nothing the
cards test). If D ≈ E, the ledger is a formatting effect.

## 2. Is the format load-bearing, or only the substance?

If the same judgment written as plain prose does the same work, the right
product is a writing guide, not a rule format. **Settled by:** the D vs F
contrast (F carries the same substance, clause for clause per F-MAPPING,
without the DECIDE/BECAUSE/UNLESS/SIGNAL shape). The preregistered decision
rule promotes a prose guide and builds no xOP-specific runtime if D ≈ F.

## 3. Does durable judgment let go?

The program's own primary risk: a ledger makes judgment durable, including
judgment whose warrant has expired. Pilot 000's card mix includes
release-stale, exception, and bypass cards, and its gate is zero incorrect
reuse on non-eligible cards — but that measures one session against frozen
cards. Whether REVIEW dates, BECAUSE-as-expiry, and supersede-not-delete
actually keep a *living* ledger honest over months of real use is untested by
any current instrument. **Settled by:** longitudinal field observation after
the pilot — if the pilot earns one.

## 4. Does a rule's judgment transfer across domains?

The pilot runs two domains (coding delivery, design critique) with
domain-specific ledgers. Whether a conduct rule authored in one domain holds
in another — or degrades into misfires — is out of scope for Pilot 000
entirely. **Settled by:** a later pilot with cross-domain application, only if
Pilot 000 passes.

## 5. Is named compliance real compliance?

The ledger instructs the model to name a rule when applying it. A model can
narrate rule-following without the artifact changing — compliance theater in
place of conduct. The labeling protocol separates the artifact axes from the
narration for this reason. **Settled by:** whether blind labels on artifacts
alone (labelers never see the model's rule citations) reproduce the D-vs-C
difference, if any.

## 6. Is any observed effect above the run-to-run floor?

Model-side variance can produce arm differences with no cause. **Settled by:**
the C′/D′ repeatability checks — an effect counts only if it exceeds what two
identical runs produce on their own. Borrowed discipline; sourced in
[RELATED-WORK.md](RELATED-WORK.md).

## 7. Is the residual at procedure depth the same quantity as at activation depth?

lyra's formula frames the gap in words this folder's README uses: inherited
default versus present warrant. Whether the thing a Judgment Ledger manages at
the level of *procedure* is continuous with what lyra measures at the level of
*activation* is the bridge question, and it is open in both directions.
**Settled by:** the bridge experiment, pre-registered in
[experiments/bridge/PROTOCOL.md](../../experiments/bridge/PROTOCOL.md) — status
`DESIGNED`, not yet run. It scores the *identical* persisting response under both
halves of a held-response-constant pair, so any difference in the coherence trace
can only have entered through the warrant. Its decision rules commit to publishing
the null: if the activation signal carries no information about warrant state, the
two depths are structurally analogous and not the same quantity, and that is the
answer. The pilot still neither tests this nor assumes it; treating the two as the
same quantity before the bridge runs would be exactly the kind of unwarranted
inherited claim the program exists to catch.
